# Literal Film User Guide

## Getting Started

1. From your Gallery (or any app), share a photo
2. Pick **Literal Film** from the share sheet
3. The photo opens straight into the editor, unfiltered

There's no import screen and nothing to configure first.

## Applying a Look

Along the bottom, your saved presets appear as thumbnails rendered against your actual photo — tap one to apply it instantly. Tap the same thumbnail again to go back to unfiltered.

Long-press the preview at any time to see the original underneath; release to return to the current look.

## Building Your Own Look

Tap the wrench icon (bottom left) to open the manual editor. It starts from whatever preset is currently selected (or a neutral base if none is).

Sliders available:

- **Brightness** — overall exposure
- **Contrast** — an S-curve; right of center adds contrast, left flattens it
- **Shadow Lift** — right lifts (opens up) shadows, left crushes them
- **Highlight Rolloff** — right brightens/expands highlights, left compresses and tames them
- **Structure** — broad local contrast
- **Sharpness** — fine detail
- **Grain** — film-grain texture
- **Vignette** — edge darkening
- **Temperature** — warm/cool color cast
- **Monochrome** — switch to black and white

The preview updates live as you drag. Long-press the preview here too, to compare against the unedited photo.

In the editor's top bar:

- **Save icon** — saves the photo as currently previewed straight to the gallery
- **Share icon** — exports the photo as currently previewed to the share sheet (e.g. to send to a PC via KDE Connect)
- **Gear icon** — saves your current slider settings as a new named preset, added to the bottom row

## Saving and Exporting

From the main screen, the same two icons appear top-right:

- **Save** writes the photo (with whichever preset is selected, or unfiltered if none is) to `Pictures/Literal Film` in your gallery
- **Export** hands the photo to the share sheet instead, without adding anything to your gallery

## Managing Presets

Long-press any preset thumbnail to delete it. Deleted presets are gone for good — the row closes up to fill the gap.
